# Adult Sepsis and Septic Shock Clinical Guideline

> SYNTHETIC DOCUMENT — FOR DEMONSTRATION ONLY. This guideline was generated for
> the Arogya P-02 hackathon build. It is not clinical advice and must not be used
> for patient care.

## 1. Purpose and Scope

### 1.1 Purpose

This guideline standardises the recognition, resuscitation and antimicrobial
management of adults (aged 18 years and older) with suspected sepsis or septic
shock across the Emergency Department (ED), inpatient wards and the Intensive
Care Unit (ICU) of the synthetic Arogya General Hospital network.

### 1.2 Scope

The guideline applies to all adult inpatients and ED attendances. Paediatric and
neonatal sepsis are out of scope and are covered by a separate paediatric
pathway that is not part of this document set. Obstetric sepsis follows this
guideline with the modifications listed in Section 8.2.

### 1.3 Relationship to Other Documents

Version 4.2 of this guideline supersedes the ED Empiric Antibiotic SOP v1.4
(DOC-SOP-ED-2021). Where the 2021 SOP and this guideline disagree, the
Antimicrobial Stewardship Committee has directed that this guideline is the
authoritative source. Dosing product details are maintained in the Hospital
Drug Formulary (DOC-FORM-2025).

## 2. Definitions

### 2.1 Sepsis

Sepsis is life-threatening organ dysfunction caused by a dysregulated host
response to infection. Organ dysfunction is identified as an acute change in
total SOFA score of 2 points or more consequent to the infection.

### 2.2 Septic Shock

Septic shock is a subset of sepsis in which profound circulatory, cellular and
metabolic abnormalities are associated with a greater risk of mortality.
Patients with septic shock are identified by a vasopressor requirement to
maintain a mean arterial pressure (MAP) of 65 mmHg or greater and a serum
lactate level greater than 2 mmol/L in the absence of hypovolaemia.

### 2.3 Time Zero

Time zero is the time at which sepsis is first recognised, defined as the
earliest documented time of a positive sepsis screen in the ED triage record or
the ward early-warning escalation record.

## 3. Recognition and Initial Resuscitation

### 3.1 Screening

All adult ED attendances must be screened with the qSOFA and NEWS2 tools at
triage. A qSOFA score of 2 or more, or a NEWS2 score of 5 or more with suspected
infection, triggers the sepsis pathway and a senior clinician review within
15 minutes.

### 3.2 Hour-1 Bundle

The following actions must be completed within 1 hour of time zero:

- Measure serum lactate; remeasure within 2 to 4 hours if the initial lactate
  is greater than 2 mmol/L.
- Obtain two sets of blood cultures before administering antibiotics, provided
  this does not delay antibiotics.
- Administer broad-spectrum antibiotics within 1 hour of sepsis recognition.
- Begin rapid administration of 30 mL/kg intravenous crystalloid for
  hypotension or lactate of 4 mmol/L or more.
- Apply vasopressors if hypotensive during or after fluid resuscitation to
  maintain MAP of 65 mmHg or greater.

### 3.3 Fluid Resuscitation

Balanced crystalloids (for example, Plasma-Lyte or Hartmann's solution) are
preferred over 0.9% saline. Fluid responsiveness should be reassessed after each
500 mL bolus using dynamic measures where available. Albumin may be considered
in patients who have received large volumes of crystalloid.

### 3.4 Vasopressors

Norepinephrine is the first-line vasopressor. Vasopressin at 0.03 units/min may
be added when norepinephrine exceeds 0.25 mcg/kg/min. Peripheral administration
of norepinephrine is permitted for up to 6 hours through a large-bore cannula
proximal to the antecubital fossa while central access is obtained.

## 4. Empiric Antimicrobial Therapy

### 4.1 General Principles

Empiric therapy is selected according to the suspected source, local
antibiogram data and patient risk factors for multidrug-resistant organisms.
All empiric regimens must be reviewed at 48 to 72 hours with culture results,
and de-escalated wherever possible.

### 4.2 Vancomycin Dosing in Adults

For adults with sepsis where MRSA coverage is indicated, vancomycin is given as
a weight-based loading dose of 25 mg/kg IV (actual body weight, maximum 3 g),
followed by a maintenance dose of 15 mg/kg IV every 12 hours. Maintenance dosing
must be adjusted to an AUC/MIC target of 400 to 600 mg·h/L using pharmacy-led
Bayesian dosing software. Trough-only monitoring is no longer recommended.

The infusion rate must not exceed 10 mg/min (1 g over at least 100 minutes) to
reduce the risk of vancomycin infusion reaction.

### 4.3 Vancomycin Monitoring and Renal Adjustment

Serum creatinine must be checked at baseline and at least every 24 to 48 hours
while on therapy. For creatinine clearance below 50 mL/min, the maintenance
interval is extended to every 24 hours and a pharmacist review is mandatory.
Concomitant piperacillin-tazobactam increases the risk of acute kidney injury
and requires daily creatinine monitoring.

### 4.4 Piperacillin-Tazobactam

For suspected intra-abdominal, urinary or unknown-source sepsis without
beta-lactam allergy, piperacillin-tazobactam 4.5 g IV every 6 hours is given as
an extended infusion over 4 hours after an initial 30-minute loading infusion.

### 4.5 Meropenem

Meropenem 1 g IV every 8 hours is reserved for patients with known or suspected
ESBL-producing organisms, recent carbapenem-susceptible multidrug-resistant
infection, or septic shock with prior broad-spectrum antibiotic exposure within
90 days. Meropenem requires Infectious Diseases (ID) approval after 72 hours.

### 4.6 Beta-Lactam Allergy

For patients with a documented severe beta-lactam allergy (anaphylaxis or
severe cutaneous reaction), aztreonam 2 g IV every 8 hours plus vancomycin is
recommended. Low-risk allergy histories should be referred for allergy
de-labelling and a beta-lactam used where appropriate.

### 4.7 Linezolid and Daptomycin

Linezolid 600 mg IV or oral every 12 hours, or daptomycin 8 to 10 mg/kg IV every
24 hours, are alternatives to vancomycin when vancomycin is contraindicated,
when the vancomycin MIC is 2 mg/L or greater, or following vancomycin-associated
acute kidney injury. Both agents are restricted and require prior authorisation
under the payer policy (DOC-PAY-2024).

## 5. Cautions and Contraindications

### 5.1 Vancomycin Infusion Reaction

Rapid vancomycin infusion can cause flushing and hypotension (vancomycin
infusion reaction). If a reaction occurs, stop the infusion, give an
antihistamine, and restart at half the previous rate once symptoms resolve.
Do not document an infusion reaction as a drug allergy.

### 5.2 Nephrotoxicity

The combination of vancomycin with aminoglycosides, loop diuretics, intravenous
contrast or piperacillin-tazobactam increases the risk of nephrotoxicity. Avoid
concurrent nephrotoxins where clinically possible.

### 5.3 Linezolid Interactions

Linezolid is a reversible monoamine oxidase inhibitor. Avoid co-administration
with serotonergic agents (for example SSRIs) because of the risk of serotonin
syndrome, and monitor platelet counts weekly for courses longer than 14 days.

## 6. Source Control and De-escalation

### 6.1 Source Control

An anatomical source requiring emergent source control (for example, an abscess
or obstructed urinary tract) should be identified and controlled within 6 to 12
hours of diagnosis where feasible.

### 6.2 Antimicrobial De-escalation

Therapy must be reviewed daily. Once a pathogen and susceptibilities are known,
therapy should be narrowed. A total treatment duration of 7 days is adequate for
most patients with adequate source control. Procalcitonin may support
discontinuation decisions but must not delay clinical judgement.

## 7. Monitoring and Escalation

### 7.1 Monitoring

Patients on the sepsis pathway require observations at least every 30 minutes
for the first 4 hours, hourly urine output measurement, and a repeat lactate
within 2 to 4 hours when the initial value is elevated.

### 7.2 ICU Referral

Refer to the ICU outreach team when MAP remains below 65 mmHg after 30 mL/kg of
crystalloid, when lactate remains at 4 mmol/L or more, or when the patient
requires vasopressor support.

## 8. Special Populations

### 8.1 Obesity

For patients with a body mass index of 40 kg/m2 or more, the vancomycin loading
dose remains capped at 3 g and maintenance dosing must be guided by pharmacy
AUC modelling rather than fixed weight-based calculation.

### 8.2 Pregnancy

Obstetric sepsis follows this guideline with urgent obstetric review within
30 minutes. Fluid resuscitation volumes may be reduced in pre-eclampsia.

## 9. Documentation and Audit

Every sepsis pathway activation must be documented with time zero, antibiotic
administration time and bundle completion status. Adverse drug events related
to antimicrobials must be reported to the pharmacovigilance system within
24 hours. Compliance with the Hour-1 Bundle is audited monthly by the Quality
and Patient Safety Office.
