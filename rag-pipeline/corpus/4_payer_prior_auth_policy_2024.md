# Payer Prior Authorization Policy for Restricted Anti-Infectives

> SYNTHETIC DOCUMENT — FOR DEMONSTRATION ONLY. The payer, plan and policy
> number are fictional.

## 1. Policy Statement

Policy PA-ANTI-117 defines the prior authorization (PA) requirements of the
synthetic payer "Arogya Community Health Plan" for restricted anti-infective
medications administered in inpatient and outpatient settings.

## 2. Medications Requiring Prior Authorization

The following medications require prior authorization before the second dose
is dispensed: linezolid, daptomycin, micafungin, tigecycline,
ceftazidime-avibactam, colistimethate and albumin 20%.

Vancomycin, piperacillin-tazobactam, meropenem, ceftriaxone and cefepime do not
require prior authorization under this policy. Meropenem use beyond 14 days
requires a medical necessity review.

## 3. Authorization Requirements

### 3.1 Required Documentation

A prior authorization request must include the diagnosis code, culture and
susceptibility results (or documented reason they are unavailable), the
proposed dose and duration, and a statement from the prescriber explaining why
a formulary alternative without prior authorization is not appropriate.

### 3.2 Turnaround Time

Urgent inpatient requests are decided within 24 hours of receipt. Standard
outpatient requests are decided within 72 hours. Requests submitted without the
required documentation are pended and the turnaround clock restarts when the
documentation is complete.

### 3.3 Approval Duration

Approvals are valid for up to 14 days for inpatient therapy and up to 28 days
for outpatient parenteral antimicrobial therapy (OPAT). Extensions require a
new request with updated clinical documentation.

## 4. Exceptions

### 4.1 Emergency Exception

The first dose of any restricted anti-infective may be administered without
prior authorization in a documented life-threatening emergency, including
septic shock. The PA request must then be submitted within 1 business day.

### 4.2 Continuity of Care

Members transferred from another facility on an approved restricted
anti-infective may continue therapy for up to 72 hours while a new request is
processed.

## 5. Denials and Appeals

A denied request may be appealed by the prescriber within 30 calendar days.
Expedited appeals for inpatients are decided within 72 hours. Billing
Specialists must attach the denial letter and appeal outcome to the claim.

## 6. Claims Submission

Claims for restricted anti-infectives must include the prior authorization
number in the claim header. Claims without a valid authorization number are
denied with reason code PA-01, except where the emergency exception in
Section 4.1 is documented.
